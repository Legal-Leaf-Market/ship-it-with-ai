Small business ERP

Every job, one record, from inquiry to paid

ERP sounds like something only big companies buy. For a small business it means one thing: when someone asks “are we all set for Saturday?”, you can answer without digging through email, a spreadsheet and your payment app.

Is this for you?

  • You run bookings, events, jobs or orders that go through several steps before they are done.
  • Signed agreements, deposits and balances live in different places.
  • You have outgrown a spreadsheet but big software is too much and too expensive.
  • More than one person needs to see where each job stands.

What you get

One ID per job

The inquiry, agreement, signature and payment all point to the same booking.

Status you can trust

A booking is confirmed only when the rules you set are met, such as a signed agreement and a confirmed deposit.

Payments from the source

A deposit counts when the payment provider confirms it, not when someone opens a link.

Out of order is fine

Real customers pay before they sign, or sign twice. The system handles both without duplicate records.

Reports from real records

See what is booked, what is owed and what came in, built on the same data your team works from.

Built in steps

We start with one booking type, run it for real, then add the next.

Proof, not promises

Crossroads Wedding Co.

We run our own wedding bookings on the software I built in-house. I am extending it into bookkeeping, which is still a work in progress.

Questions people ask

What does ERP mean for a business my size?

Enterprise resource planning is a big name for keeping the main records of your business in one connected place. For a small business that is usually customers, jobs or bookings, agreements, payments and a few reports.

Why not just buy software off the shelf?

Often you should, and I will tell you when. Custom makes sense when your process is what sets you apart, or when you are paying for three tools that do not talk to each other.

Can it connect to my accounting software?

That is my background. I have spent more than ten years in accounting and technology, including connecting transaction feeds to Sage Intacct and NetSuite. We scope any accounting connection before building it.

Can I try it before committing?

Yes. You can click through the sample booking demo now, and on a project I show you a working preview on a real task before it goes into use.

Ready when you are.

A line or two is plenty. I’ll get back to you about what I can do, how long it might take, and what it would cost.
