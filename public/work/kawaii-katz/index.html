All work

Kawaii Katz · By Jake Kennedy

Ada’s Picks started at 11 p.m.

My daughter had the idea. She directed the whole project. I did my best to build the site she had in mind.

That Tuesday night

It was 11 p.m. on a Tuesday when Ada told me she wanted a website for kids. I’ll never forget it. She was ten, with her eleventh birthday coming up in February. I stayed up all night putting together a draft so she’d have something to react to.

Her first idea was big enough to be ten different websites. Together we narrowed it down to a kawaii storefront. Kawaii Katz grew out of that, and Ada’s Picks became the shelf she curates herself. I built the store and set up RSS feeds for Pinterest, including one just for her picks.

Kawaii Katz has a little corner at Legal Leaf Cafe & Market now, too. I want Ada to choose the finds we keep in stock. The idea is that shoppers can grab something off her shelf, order at the kiosk for pickup on their next visit or have it shipped home. They can also take the web address with them and browse later.

What building it did for us

We frustrated each other. I’d show her what I’d built, she’d tell me what she wanted, and we’d try again. We stuck with it, and building something together brought us closer. It was one of the best things that’s happened to our relationship.

Now, as she’s growing up and things are changing, she talks to me about what she’s going through. I love that she feels she can tell me. And it all started with her idea on a Tuesday night.
