Match card payouts to the bank

Why a processor payout never equals the day’s sales, and a simple way to tie every payout to a bank deposit. Example numbers only. Check your setup with your accountant.

On this page

  1. See why the numbers differ
  2. Pull the payout report
  3. Break a payout into its parts
  4. Use a clearing account
  5. Match deposits by payout
  6. Chase what doesn’t tie out

1. See why the numbers differ

Your card processor does not send you each sale. It groups sales into a payout, subtracts its fees, refunds and any disputes, and sends one deposit a day or two later. So Tuesday’s sales report and Thursday’s bank deposit rarely show the same number, even when nothing is wrong.

2. Pull the payout report

Every major processor, including Square, Stripe and Shopify Payments, has a payouts or transfers report. Export it for the month with one row per payout, and ask for the detail that lists the transactions inside each payout. Keep the payout ID. It is the thread that ties everything together.

3. Break a payout into its parts

Write each payout as gross sales minus everything taken out. These numbers are an example.

Payout P-1042Amount
Gross card sales$1,240.00
Processing fees−$36.21
Refunds−$45.00
Dispute withheld−$60.00
Net payout to bank$1,098.79

If the parts don’t add up to the net payout, stop here. Something is missing from the export, usually an adjustment or a reserve.

4. Use a clearing account

A clearing account is a temporary holding account in your books, often called something like “Card payouts clearing”. Record sales into it at their gross amount when they happen. When the payout lands, record the fees and refunds against it and move the net to the bank. When every payout has landed, the clearing account goes back to zero.

Day of sale:   Clearing        1,240.00   Sales 1,240.00
Payout lands:  Bank            1,098.79
               Card fees          36.21
               Refunds            45.00
               Disputes held      60.00
                                Clearing 1,240.00

Your accountant may name these accounts differently. Ask them before you set it up.

5. Match deposits by payout

In your bank feed, match each deposit to exactly one payout ID by amount and date. Processors often deposit a payout a business day or two after it is created, so allow for weekends and holidays. Never match a deposit to a day of sales. Match it to the payout.

6. Chase what doesn’t tie out

At month end, anything left in the clearing account is a question. A positive balance usually means a payout has not landed yet, which is normal for the last day or two. An old balance points to a missing payout, a refund recorded twice or a fee that was never booked. Write down what each leftover item is before you close the month.

This is the kind of check I build into tools for clients: pull the payouts, break them into parts, and flag the ones that do not tie out.
