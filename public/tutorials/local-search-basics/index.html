Get found in local search

The pages, listings and checks that help nearby customers find a small business. Uses a fictional furniture repair shop as the example.

On this page

  1. Claim your business profile
  2. Keep your name, address and phone identical
  3. Give each service its own page
  4. Write titles people click
  5. Tell search engines what is on the page
  6. Check what people actually search

1. Claim your business profile

For a business with a storefront or a service area, the map results often matter more than the website. Claim your free Google Business Profile and the matching listing in Apple Business Connect and Bing Places. Choose the most specific primary category that is true, such as “Barber shop” rather than “Beauty salon”.

Fill in hours, including holiday hours, your service area if you travel to customers, and a link to the page where people can book or order. Add real photos of your place and your work.

2. Keep your name, address and phone identical

Search engines compare your business details across the web. Pick one exact form of your name, address and phone number and use it everywhere: your website footer, your profiles, your social pages and directories.

Name: Mill Street Furniture Repair (fictional)
Address: 100 Example St, Columbus, IN 47201
Phone: 812-555-0100
Same spelling, same suite number, same phone, everywhere.

When you move or change your phone number, update every listing the same week.

3. Give each service its own page

One page that lists everything you do can only rank for so much. Give each main service its own page that says what it is, who it is for, the area you serve and how to book. A furniture repair shop might have pages for chair repair, refinishing and pickup.

Write for customers, not for search engines. Don’t make a near copy of the same page for every nearby town. Thin pages like that tend to hurt more than they help.

4. Write titles people click

The page title is usually the blue link people see in results. Lead with what you do and where, then your name. Keep it short enough to show in full. The meta description is the short summary under it. It does not set your rank, but a clear one earns more clicks.

<title>Chair Repair in Columbus, IN | Mill Street Furniture Repair</title>
<meta name="description" content="Loose joints, broken seats and
wood touch-ups. Free pickup estimates in Columbus, Indiana.">

Every page gets its own title and description. Repeating the same one across the site is a common mistake.

5. Tell search engines what is on the page

Structured data is a small block of code that states facts the page already shows, such as your business name, hours and address, in a form search engines read reliably. Only mark up what a visitor can see on the page.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{"@context":"https://schema.org","@type":"LocalBusiness",
 "name":"Mill Street Furniture Repair",
 "address":{"@type":"PostalAddress","streetAddress":"100 Example St",
  "addressLocality":"Columbus","addressRegion":"IN","postalCode":"47201"},
 "telephone":"812-555-0100","url":"https://example.com/"}
</script>

Also publish a sitemap.xml listing every page you want found, mention it in robots.txt, and give each page a canonical link to its own address.

6. Check what people actually search

Add your site to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools and submit the sitemap. After a few weeks the performance report shows the searches that brought people to you and the pages they landed on. That list is your best guide to what page to write next.

Ask happy customers to leave a review in their own words, and reply to reviews you get. Never buy reviews or write them yourself. Review platforms remove fake reviews and can penalize the listing.
